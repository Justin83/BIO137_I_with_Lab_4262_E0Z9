Chapters in This Video

  • Introduction: Bone Tissue and Bone Growth
    Introduces the recording as a brief Chapter 6 review of bone tissue and growth before the axial and appendicular skeleton.
  • Functions of the Skeletal System
    Reviews support, movement, protection, mineral storage, blood-cell production, and lipid storage.
  • Cartilage and Hyaline Bone Templates
    Explains how hyaline cartilage serves as a developmental template for many bones.
  • Chondroblasts, Chondrocytes, Lacunae, and Perichondrium
    Distinguishes cartilage cells, extracellular matrix, lacunae, and the surrounding perichondrium.
  • Articular Cartilage
    Describes smooth cartilage at joint surfaces and its role in reducing friction.
  • Appositional and Interstitial Cartilage Growth
    Compares growth from the surface with growth from within the cartilage matrix.
  • Bone Matrix and Hydroxyapatite
    Reviews the inorganic mineral portion of bone matrix and the importance of calcium phosphate crystals.
  • Osteoblasts, Osteocytes, and Osteoclasts
    Introduces the major bone-cell types and their roles in formation, maintenance, and resorption.
  • Important correction in the older recording
    At about 6:02, the terms are reversed. Osteoblasts build bone and can become osteocytes; osteocytes are mature bone cells located in lacunae.
  • How Osteoclasts Resorb Bone
    Uses the ruffled border to explain how osteoclasts break down mineralized bone.
  • Bone Cell Review
    Summarizes the differences among osteoblasts, osteocytes, and osteoclasts.
  • Osteochondral Progenitor Cells
    Explains how progenitor cells can develop into osteoblasts or chondroblasts.
  • Woven Bone vs. Lamellar Bone
    Contrasts immature, less-organized woven bone with mature, layered lamellar bone.
  • Spongy Bone and Trabeculae
    Describes trabeculae, marrow spaces, reduced weight, and alignment along lines of stress.
  • Compact Bone, Osteons, and Lamellae
    Introduces osteons, concentric lamellae, interstitial lamellae, and circumferential lamellae.

What to Watch For

This is an older instructor recording from an earlier version of the course. It is provided only as an optional review. Watch it at your choosing; it is not a new graded assignment and does not replace the current Module 3 materials.

Use it to refresh the foundations behind the skeletal system: cartilage growth, bone matrix and hydroxyapatite, the roles of osteoblasts, osteocytes, and osteoclasts, and the organization of woven, lamellar, spongy, and compact bone.

The most important correction is at about 6:02: osteoblasts are bone-forming cells that can become osteocytes. Osteocytes are mature bone cells housed in lacunae.

This review is most useful before or alongside the current skeletal-system work, especially when the newer material assumes you remember how bone tissue is built and remodeled.
